SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE DOCUMENT -- Generic Safety Procedure
Title: Confined Space Entry Procedure

Purpose
This generic procedure describes the minimum requirements for safe entry into a confined space (such as a storage tank, vessel, or below-grade pit) where the atmosphere may be hazardous or oxygen-deficient. Synthetic and illustrative only.

Definitions
A confined space, for this procedure, is any space large enough for a person to enter, with limited means of entry/exit, that is not designed for continuous occupancy -- for example the interior of Storage Tank TK-600 during an out-of-service inspection.

Required steps before entry
1. Obtain a confined space entry permit, valid for a single shift only, signed by an authorized permit issuer.
2. Isolate the space from all hazardous energy per the Lockout-Tagout procedure (electrical, mechanical, and process isolation including blinding or double block-and-bleed of process connections).
3. Ventilate the space mechanically and test the atmosphere before entry for: oxygen (must read 19.5% to 23.5%), flammable gas (must read below 10% of the lower explosive limit), and toxic gases relevant to the space's service (e.g. H2S, must read below the applicable exposure limit).
4. Continue atmospheric monitoring throughout the entry, not just before it.
5. Station a dedicated attendant outside the space at all times entry is in progress; the attendant does not enter the space and maintains communication with the entrant(s).
6. Ensure rescue equipment (retrieval line/harness, and a means to summon rescue) is in place before entry begins.

PPE and equipment
Entrants wear, at minimum, the PPE specified in the PPE Requirements procedure for the specific hazards of the space, plus a full-body harness with retrieval line for vertical entries.

Emergency response
If the atmosphere alarm activates or an entrant reports distress, all entrants evacuate immediately; the attendant does not enter the space to attempt rescue and instead activates the emergency response plan.

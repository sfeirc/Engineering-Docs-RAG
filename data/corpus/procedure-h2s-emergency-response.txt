SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE DOCUMENT -- Generic Safety Procedure
Title: H2S (Hydrogen Sulfide) Emergency Response Procedure

Purpose
This generic procedure describes the immediate response actions to a confirmed or suspected hydrogen sulfide (H2S) release. Synthetic and illustrative only.

Background
H2S is a toxic, flammable, colorless gas that can be present in some hydrocarbon streams. It is dangerous at concentrations far below its flammable range and its odor (rotten-egg smell) cannot be relied upon for warning at higher, more dangerous concentrations because it deadens the sense of smell.

Immediate actions on alarm
1. On any personal H2S monitor alarm or fixed detector alarm (see Fire and Gas Detection System datasheet for setpoints), immediately move upwind and to a higher elevation if possible, and do not attempt to identify the source.
2. Sound the area alarm / notify the control room using the nearest means available.
3. Do not re-enter the affected area without appropriate supplied-air respiratory protection and authorization from the incident controller.
4. Account for all personnel at the designated muster point.

First aid
A person overcome by H2S must be removed from the affected area by rescuers wearing appropriate breathing apparatus -- a would-be rescuer without breathing apparatus becomes a second casualty and must not enter. Once in fresh air, administer first aid/CPR as trained and required, and ensure emergency medical services are called immediately.

Relevant equipment
Areas with H2S potential (for example around Compressor RC-200 and its associated piping) are covered by the fixed H2S detection described in the Fire and Gas Detection System datasheet, with alarm and evacuation setpoints as stated there.

This is a synthetic example procedure created for a retrieval-augmented-generation demo corpus. It is illustrative only and must not be used as an actual operating instruction.

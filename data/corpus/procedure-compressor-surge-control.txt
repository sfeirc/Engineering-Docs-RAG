Title: Centrifugal Compressor Surge Control and Prevention Procedure

Purpose
This generic procedure describes the operating philosophy that keeps a centrifugal compressor (such as Compressor CC-300) away from surge -- an unstable flow-reversal condition that can severely damage the compressor in seconds. Synthetic and illustrative only.

Background
Surge occurs when a centrifugal compressor's flow drops below the minimum stable value for its current operating speed and discharge pressure, causing a rapid flow reversal through the machine. It is mechanically damaging (thrust bearing loading, blade stress) and must be prevented, not merely detected after the fact.

Prevention philosophy
1. An anti-surge control system continuously compares the current operating point (flow and head/pressure ratio) against a surge control line held at a safety margin from the actual measured surge line (see Compressor CC-300 datasheet for the stated margin).
2. As the operating point approaches the surge control line, the anti-surge valve (Control Valve CV-700) opens automatically to recycle flow and move the operating point away from surge, before the true surge line is reached.
3. The anti-surge valve's fast full-stroke time is a deliberate design choice: surge can develop faster than a slow-acting valve could respond.

Operator actions
1. Do not disable or bypass the anti-surge control system to "improve" apparent efficiency; the surge margin exists precisely because the surge line measured at the factory does not perfectly match every real operating and fouling condition.
2. Any surge event that does occur (recognized by a rapid, audible flow reversal and pressure/flow oscillation) requires an immediate reduction in compressor speed or load and a subsequent investigation before returning to the prior operating point.
